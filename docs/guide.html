Get started in two minutes

The order that protects you best.

  1. Install Tijori and tap Create my Tijori.
  2. Pick a passphrase: Tijori suggests 5 random words. Write them on paper and keep it at home.
  3. Tap Back up to Google Drive when asked (details below).
  4. On the last screen, tap Import from Google Password Manager, or Add my Gmail first.

Your email account resets every other password. Give it your strongest password and turn on 2-step verification.

Turn on Google Drive backup

Tijori keeps one file, Tijori-backup.tijori, in your Drive and updates it a few seconds after every change. Google can store it but can’t read it.

  1. Open Settings → Google Drive backup (or do it during setup).
  2. Tap Back up to Google Drive. Android’s “Save to” screen opens.
  3. Tap ☰ at the top-left and choose Drive.
  4. Open a folder, or stay in My Drive, and tap Save.
  5. Back in Tijori you’ll see Backed up, with the time. That’s it.

Don’t see Drive in the list? Install the Google Drive app from Play Store and sign in with your Gmail, then try again.

Uninstalled? Get everything back

Works the same after a phone reset or on a new phone.

  1. Install Tijori again from Google Play.
  2. On the first screen tap Restore from Google Drive.
  3. Tap Find my backup, then ☰ → Drive → Tijori-backup.tijori.
  4. Type your master passphrase and tap Restore my vault.
  5. Leave Keep backing up to this file ticked so backups carry on.

If you turned off “Restore with passphrase only”, step 4 also asks for the Secret Key printed on your Emergency Kit.

Moving to a new phone

  1. On the new phone, follow the reinstall steps above.
  2. Turn fingerprint unlock on again in Settings: it is tied to each phone’s secure chip.
  3. Choose Tijori as the autofill service again (see below).
  4. On the old phone, open Settings → Erase this vault once the new phone works. Your Drive file is untouched.

Import from Google Password Manager

Bring your existing passwords across in one go.

  1. Open Google Password Manager (Android Settings → Google → Autofill → Google Password Manager, or passwords.google.com on a computer).
  2. Tap ⚙ Settings → Export passwords → Export and save the file to Downloads.
  3. In Tijori open Settings → Import passwords → Choose file and pick that file.
  4. Check the preview and tap Import. Passwords already in Tijori are skipped.
  5. Delete the exported file from Downloads (and your laptop).

The exported file isn’t encrypted. Anyone who opens it can read every password, so delete it right after importing.

Fill passwords in apps and Chrome

  1. In Tijori open Settings → Autofill service and choose Tijori.
  2. In Chrome tap ⋮ → Settings → Autofill services and pick Autofill using another service, then restart Chrome.
  3. Tap any login box, then the Tijori suggestion, and unlock.

Tijori fills only on the exact app or website you saved. If it doesn’t offer to fill a site you trust, open the login in Tijori and check its Website field.

The three rooms

Everyday holds email, shopping, social and Wi-Fi, and opens with the vault. Money holds banking, cards, UPI and investments; it asks for your fingerprint every time and locks itself after a minute. Family holds insurance, locker and will details, which your nominees can open with their cards.

For cards, save only the last 4 digits and a PIN hint. Never save a real PIN, CVV or OTP anywhere.

Nominee cards for your family

  1. Open the Family tab and choose how many cards (2 of 3 is usual).
  2. Print the cards and give one each to people you trust.
  3. Save the Family package somewhere they can reach, for example a shared Drive folder.
  4. Do the practice drill together once so they know what to do.

One card alone reveals nothing. If a card is lost, tap “Cancel all cards” and make a new set.

Forgot your passphrase?

Nobody can reset it, not even us: that’s what keeps your vault safe. Try variations first: capital letters, the spaces between words, an older passphrase. If nominee cards exist, your family can still open the Family room. Otherwise the vault can’t be opened, and you start a new one.

Keep your passphrase written on paper at home, next to your important documents.
